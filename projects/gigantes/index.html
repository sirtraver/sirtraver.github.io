Visual design · 2019

Gigantes

A photographic series on the presence of architecture in the city. Buildings are viewed from below, turning façades, lines and reflections into compositions of scale and perspective.

Discipline
Visual design
Year
2019

The project

Gigantes brings together buildings of different appearances and periods through one way of looking. Framing starts at street level and directs attention upwards. Perspective, sky and repeated lines connect the different architectures.

The idea

The building’s scale changes as the street context almost disappears. Façades become planes that converge, curve or cut into the sky. Photography explores this transformation without requiring the buildings to share a style.

The series

Images alternate glass, stone, smooth surfaces and ornament. The viewpoint maintains continuity, while changes in material and geometry introduce variation. The sequence invites comparisons of shape and proportion rather than a catalogue of buildings.

Design language

Framing and perspective

Low viewpoints make vertical lines converge and emphasise height. Some frames use a central axis; others shift the building towards a side or corner. The relationship between façade and image edge controls how much sky remains and where tension gathers.

Light, colour and surface

Blue skies and reflections connect images of different materials. Clouds introduce variation against repeated windows and modules. On lighter façades, light reveals relief and ornament; in glass, it brings architecture and sky together on one surface.

Visual sequence

The repeated viewpoint makes the photographs read as a series. Changes between symmetrical and diagonal compositions, light and dark buildings, and curved or straight surfaces introduce variation. Each image stands on its own while finding formal relationships with its neighbours.

Result

A series that turns architectural observation into a compositional exercise. Perspective, light and repetition connect different buildings and reveal a shared way of looking at the city.
