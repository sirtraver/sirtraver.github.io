BRASA

Spec work — not affiliated with any real brand. A full launch for an invented hot sauce brand in three heat levels: strategy, range naming, identity and brand guidelines, labels, gift box, street and social campaign, product page, email, banners and two 10-second spots.

Discipline
Visual design
Year
2026
Role
Brand strategy, naming, identity and label design, campaign concept and copywriting, channel plan, art direction, AI image and video generation, layout of the pieces, web and email
Deliverables
Strategy: category, positioning and insight, Range naming and heat-level system, Wordmark and brand guidelines, Three labels as final artwork, and a gift box, Packshot and product photography, Poster triptych and street panels in Spanish and English, Social pieces (1:1, 9:16, 16:9), Product page, email and display banners, 10-second spots in 9:16 and 16:9, Channel and measurement plan
Tools
Google Flow · Nano Banana 2.1 · Omni 1.2 Flash · Claude

The challenge

A small hot sauce kitchen that sells unbranded at farmers’ markets wants to get into delis and open an online shop. People who try the sauces come back, but on a shelf or a website there is no one to explain which one to pick or how hot each one is.

Discovery

The shelf is split in two: traditional sauces, with dense labels that assume you already know them, and “extreme” sauces with skulls, flames and Scoville numbers, all very alike. Heat level is the buyer’s first question and it is rarely answered well.

Insight

“I like heat, but I don’t want to prove anything: I want to know how hot it is before I pour it.” The category talks about bravery; the buyer talks about control.

Strategy

BRASA sits in the empty spot on the map: frank but not threatening, with the heat explained without reading. The promise is one sentence: BRASA tells you how hot it is, no nonsense. The personality is direct with a dry sense of humour, never cocky.

Range and identity

Three levels whose names already state the intensity (CHISPA, LLAMA, INCENDIO: spark, flame, blaze), three colours and three dots that fill up. Three routes were explored; “Scale” was chosen because it is the only one that answers “how hot?” without reading, and it prints in one ink. Everything is set out in brand guidelines.

The idea

“Hot, no nonsense.” Each level is introduced with a line that says exactly how hot it is: “Hot, just a bit”, “Hot, properly”, “Hot. Full stop”. The brand warns you once, at the end of every piece: “You were warned”.

Campaign and channels

Three phases: a social teaser; a launch in the street (a triptych and panels that read as a scale as you walk by), social with one level per week, display and email; then an always-on phase in the online shop with “The full scale” gift box. The product page follows the same logic: pick your level.

Objectives and measurement

As a spec project there are no market results, and none are made up. The plan sets measurable targets: at least 80% picking the right level without reading in a 5-second test before printing, the gift box making up 30% of online orders, and 15 delis in six months.

AI production

The labels were designed first as vector artwork and given to the AI as a reference, so the bottles wear the real label. Every photo and both spots start from one range image, with two takes per piece. Small text was corrected by hand and headlines were laid out afterwards, never generated.

Design language

The wordmark

BRASA is set in an extra-bold condensed sans, rotated and running the full height of the label. On a narrow bottle, setting it vertically is how the name becomes the biggest thing on the shelf.

Three levels

Each sauce has its own colour for label, cap and backdrop: yellow for CHISPA, red for LLAMA, black for INCENDIO. The three dots in the corner fill up with the level, so the heat reads from a distance, without reading.

Campaign system

Every piece shares the same grid: a header with the number and the dots, a two-line headline at the top left, the product on its level’s colour and the “You were warned” sign-off at the bottom right. It works the same on a poster, street panel, story, banner and product page.

Colour palette

Red is the brand colour: the wordmark, the headlines and the middle sauce. Yellow and charcoal mark the other two levels. Cream is the paper: the backdrop for the range, the text on red and the inside of the box.

Result

Concept project. Spec work: BRASA is an invented brand and product, not affiliated with any real company. It shows a complete launch handled like a real commission, from strategy and naming to the website and the spots, with a three-level system that needs no explanation and the same product in every piece.
