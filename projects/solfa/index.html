Visual design · 2026 · Concept project

SOLFA

Spec work — not affiliated with any real brand. A full launch for an invented sparkling lemon soda: strategy, naming, identity and brand guidelines, can and a three-flavour range, a summer campaign in the street and on social, terrace materials, product page, email, banners and a 15-second spot.

Discipline
Visual design
Year
2026
Role
Brand strategy, naming, identity, can and range design, campaign concept and copywriting, channel plan, art direction, AI image and video generation, layout of the pieces, point-of-sale materials, web and email
Deliverables
Strategy: category, positioning and insight, Naming, Wordmark and brand guidelines, Can and three-flavour range, Packshots and terrace photography, Key visual and social pieces in Spanish and English, Street panel series and street mock-up, Coasters and table card for terraces, Product page, email and display banners, 15-second 9:16 spot, Channel and measurement plan
Tools

The challenge

A small soft-drink maker on the Mediterranean coast has a good sparkling lemon recipe, with real juice and little sugar, and sells it unbranded to a few bars. Every terrace fridge holds five near-identical lemon sodas, and nobody asks for one by name: they ask for “a lemon one”.

Discovery

Every lemon soda shows lemons, droplets and splashes, and sells itself for midday thirst. None of them owns a moment. Yet real consumption is social and happens in the evening: terraces, rooftops and music.

Insight

“At six in the evening you are not thirsty: you just want the afternoon to last.” An evening soda does not quench thirst; it makes the moment longer.

Strategy

SOLFA is positioned as the golden-hour soda: simple, and owning a moment. The promise: opening a SOLFA turns up the evening sun. The name joins sol (sun) and solfa (sol-fa, the musical scale), which gives the line “Turn up the sun” and a range that works as a scale: do, re, mi.

Identity and range

Of three routes (Sun, Lemon grove and Score), the simplest was chosen: a single-colour can, a red circle that is both the sun and a musical note, and the name standing upright. The range grows by changing only the colour: Lemon, Grapefruit and Tangerine. Everything is set out in brand guidelines.

The idea

“Turn up the sun.” Opening the can turns up the volume of the evening. The campaign works like a scale: in the street, three panels in a row where the sun rises from one to the next: “Turn up the sun”, “A little more”, “That’s it”.

Campaign and terraces

Bars and restaurants are the main channel, so the campaign reaches the table: coasters that are the sun itself and a table card with the scale, so customers ask for “the sun one”. A social teaser, street panels in terrace areas, display, email and a product page complete the summer.

Objectives and measurement

As a spec project there are no market results, and none are made up. The plan sets measurable targets: 150 terraces in the first summer, Grapefruit and Tangerine making up 25% of sales, and at least 60% linking the can to “sun” or “evening” in a 5-second test.

AI production

One reference image fixed the can and every piece comes from it. The two new flavours were first designed as flat artwork and given to the AI together with the can, so the range, terrace and street shots wear the real label. Headlines, panels, terrace kit and web were laid out afterwards, never generated.

Design language

The can

A slim 330 ml can in matte butter yellow. The name runs vertically in a very heavy geometric sans and fills almost the full height: it reads from a distance and leaves the top free for the red circle and the flavour.

Art direction

Hard late-afternoon light, long crisp shadows and single-colour backdrops. The packshot on white meets online shop standards; the terrace and rooftop scenes carry the same light into a Mediterranean setting.

System of pieces

One layout for every format: black headline top left, brand line below and a single red sun per piece, either the can’s or a large one behind it. On the street panels the sun becomes the hero and rises from one to the next; on the story the headline turns white against the sky.

Colour palette

Yellow is both the product and the backdrop; red is the sun and appears only once per piece; black carries the name and the headlines. Grapefruit and tangerine are the can colours of the other two flavours.

Result

Concept project. Spec work: SOLFA is an invented brand, not affiliated with any real company. It shows a complete launch handled like a real commission, from strategy and naming to the range, the terraces, the website and the spot, with the same can in every piece.
