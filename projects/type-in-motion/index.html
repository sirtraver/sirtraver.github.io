Visual design · 2019

Type in Motion

A typographic study based on “Subterranean Homesick Blues”. The composition explores the rhythm of words, line breaks and the contrast between white text and a black ground.

Discipline
Visual design
Year
2019

The project

The exercise reduces composition to words and space. Without supporting imagery or a broad colour range, hierarchy depends on typography itself. The presented piece shows how size, position and relationships between lines change the reading of a title.

The idea

The three words occupy separate lines with offset alignments. Reading moves diagonally rather than through a uniform rectangular block. This stepped arrangement introduces rhythm and makes empty space an active part of the composition.

The piece

The presentation focuses attention on a high-contrast composition. Serifs, letter proportions and spacing between words carry its character. The title’s rhythm and its relationship with space lead the piece.

Design language

Typographic character

The serif uses distinct terminals and changes in stroke weight to give the text texture. Letters have enough presence to function as an image. The focus is on their silhouette and the relationships between words rather than continuous reading.

Hierarchy and rhythm

Changes in word length and horizontal position create a visual sequence. The composition uses these differences rather than equalising the lines. The final word closes the arrangement, allowing the eye to move through the whole in several steps.

Contrast and space

Black and white remove colour distractions and clearly separate figure from ground. Gaps between lines and around the block control the tension. With so few elements, a change in position or scale has a visible effect on the whole.

Result

A composition that uses typography as visual material. Contrast, stepped positioning and space create a rhythmic reading from a minimal set of elements.
