COLORSPLASH

A concept identity for a paint brand. Colour moves beyond a product characteristic to become the structure of the brand, from packaging to campaign.

Discipline
Visual identity
Year
2026
Deliverables
Visual identity, Packaging, Brand communication, Retail application, E-commerce packshot, AI product photography, Social pieces
Tools
Affinity · ChatGPT · Higgsfield · Nano Banana 2 · Google Flow · Omni 1.2 Flash · Claude

The project

The proposal explores a paint brand that reads as a family while distinguishing each colour in the range. Identity, packaging and communication share a common logic: a stable mark within an environment that changes colour.

The idea

The CS monogram reduces the name to a bold geometric form. Cyan, magenta, yellow and black introduce variety without fragmenting the identity. The line “Paint it” carries this logic into spaces: the product is shown in relation to the interiors it could transform.

Applications

The tins keep the mark, colour name and code in consistent positions. A presentation box brings the range together, while the display uses colour to organise the retail setting. The campaign adapts the same scheme to four scenes, making the family recognisable without repeating the exact same image.

Campaign extension

In 2026 the “Paint it” campaign was extended with AI-generated imagery built from the brand’s own tins: the e-commerce packshot, the four colours splashing out of their tins, a drop of cyan, a half-painted magenta wall and a black brushstroke. Headlines were laid out afterwards following the original campaign. A 10-second 16:9 spot completes the extension: a roller covers a white wall in magenta and the four colours burst out of their tins.

Design language

Typography

The typography board pairs Alfa Block with Arial Rounded. Alfa Block’s dense volumes and curves echo the monogram, while Arial Rounded’s more open forms support the name and secondary information. The contrast in weight separates campaign messages from product details.

Geometry and hierarchy

The monogram combines broad curves, straight cuts and compact interior spaces. Its weight lets it lead against coloured backgrounds. Compositions separate three levels: symbol, message and information. Keeping this hierarchy prevents the different colours from turning each application into a different identity.

Art direction

Interiors and product compositions share warm lighting and textured surfaces. Campaign scenes change colour but retain a recognisable relationship between space, typography and packaging. The art direction presents colour as an experience of space as well as a characteristic of the packaging.

Colour palette

Cyan, magenta, yellow and black act as four visual families. The light mark unifies the packaging, while the dominant colour distinguishes each item. In communication, the palette extends into the depicted space so that product and scene form one composition.

Result

Concept project. It shows how one monogram and four colours are enough to organise an entire product range: the same system works on the can, the box, the campaign and the in-store display.
