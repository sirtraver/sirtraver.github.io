Visual design · 2019

MORE+

An exploration of augmented reality in graphic design and printed posters. MORE+ connects a flat composition with three-dimensional forms that extend its reading through a screen.

Discipline
Visual design
Year
2019

The project

The work treats posters as the starting point for an experience in another dimension. Printed pieces work on their own while maintaining a clear relationship with their three-dimensional interpretation. The presentation includes identity, posters, models and captures of the experience.

The idea

The “+” in the name suggests an additional layer of reading. Circles and rectangles form simple compositions that translate into volumes. The relationship between surface and object is central: the digital experience extends the printed graphics rather than acting as a separate piece.

Presentation

Posters show variations of the composition, while model views allow their elements to be compared. Phone captures document the relationship between print and screen in the project presentation.

Design language

Shape and volume

Circles and rectangles act as modules within one family. Simple geometry makes it easier to recognise which poster element becomes each volume. Changes in scale, position and overlap introduce variations while preserving the relationship between the flat composition and its spatial development.

Typography and hierarchy

The name uses a heavy, slanted sans serif to convey direction and energy. On posters it acts as a secondary signature beside the geometric composition. This separates identification from the content being transformed, preventing both levels from competing for the same space.

Colour and rhythm

Cyan, magenta, black and white organise the series. Patterns and colour fields add rhythm around the main shapes. The three-dimensional models retain the same range, linking both representations as viewpoint and depth change.

Result

An exercise in continuity between printed graphics and digital representation. Geometry, colour and repetition make it possible to follow the poster elements as they become three-dimensional.
