OYE

Spec work — not affiliated with any real brand. A full launch for an invented pebble-shaped portable speaker: strategy, naming, object design and three colourways, identity and brand guidelines, box, street and social campaign, product page, email, banners and two 15-second spots.

Discipline
Visual design
Year
2026
Role
Brand strategy, naming, concept product design, identity and packaging, campaign concept and copywriting, channel plan, art direction, AI image and video generation, layout of the pieces, web and email
Deliverables
Strategy: category, positioning and insight, Naming, Object design and three colourways, Wordmark and brand guidelines, Box with sleeve and dieline, Packshot and lifestyle photography, Key visual and social pieces in Spanish and English, Street panel series and street mock-up, Product page, email and display banners, 15-second spots in 9:16 and 16:9, Channel and measurement plan
Tools

The challenge

An audio start-up has the sound of a portable speaker worked out, but nothing else: the object, the name, the brand and the launch. The category competes on power and bass; a small speaker cannot win that fight. The client wants a speaker people actually want to leave out in view.

Discovery

Portable speakers look like sports gear: plastic cylinders, lights and numbers. The good-looking ones are fixed, expensive and wired. No one designs for the many hours a day the speaker sits switched off on a shelf.

Insight

“A speaker spends far more time being looked at than being heard.” What people really buy is how it looks at home during the hours it is off.

Strategy

OYE takes the gap between gadget and furniture: portable and an object at once. The promise fits in one line: OYE goes unnoticed until it plays. The name is what anyone says in Spanish when they want you to listen to something (“hey, listen”), and the line comes straight from it: “Hear this”.

Object and identity

Two versions of the object were designed and a river pebble was chosen, with colour only on the button and the base band. Of three identity routes, “Pebble” was chosen: a single-stroke wordmark, the same line that is debossed on the fabric. Three colourways with one rule: a neutral and an accent. Everything is set out in brand guidelines.

The idea

The speaker that hides. OYE blends into every image as one more stone, on a beach, on a shelf or by a lake, and the campaign invites you to find it: “One of these stones plays music”. Until it plays: “Hear this”.

Campaign and channels

A social teaser with no explanation; a launch with street panels in design districts, spots, display, design press and an email to the waiting list; then an always-on phase in the online shop and homeware stores with “Pick your stone”. The box repeats the product rule: a neutral sleeve over a box in the accent colour.

Objectives and measurement

As a spec project there are no market results, and none are made up. The plan sets measurable targets: at least half of people not recognising it as a speaker in a 5-second test with the living-room photo, Charcoal and Sand making up 35% of sales, and 40 design stores in the first year.

AI production

The object was fixed first in one reference image and every photo and both spots come from it, so the shape, fabric and button never change. A packshot with an unrequested plinth was redone. The box and street shots were generated from the real sleeve artwork; headlines, panels, web and pieces were laid out afterwards.

Design language

The object

A slightly asymmetric river pebble wrapped in stone-grey knit fabric. An ultramarine silicone band at the base and a single button in the same blue: all the product’s colour sits in those two places. The wordmark is lowercase and debossed, the same colour as the fabric.

Three colourways

The range repeats the same rule: a neutral fabric and one bright colour on the band and button. Stone and ultramarine, charcoal and orange, sand and sage. Each version stands apart without changing the shape.

Box and pieces

The box is rigid, in the accent colour, with an off-white paper sleeve printed in one ink: neutral outside and colour at the edges, like the speaker. On the pieces the line sits top left over the quiet part of the image and the sign-off at the bottom; only the key visual centres the headline in white on ultramarine.

Colour palette

Stone grey is the product and off-white is the space. Ultramarine is the only bright colour: it marks the button, the band and the key visual backdrop. The other two versions swap that blue for orange or sage.

Result

Concept project. Spec work: OYE is an invented brand and product, not affiliated with any real company. It shows a complete launch handled like a real commission, from strategy and object design to the box, the website and the spots, with the same product in every piece.
